2. Where it lands

The first screen on her phone

weareplufl.com/pages/hugl-sleep-system-advertorial

Since 2 June the page has split its headline three ways, 34/33/33, set by page only. Which one she reads depends on the visitor, not on the ad she tapped.

3. The break

Two in three night sweats clicks read a headline that doesn't use the ad's words.

Version 2 is the only one that says hot sleepers. Versions 1 and 3 never mention night sweats or hot sleepers.

Version 3 opens on back and hip pain, then the subhead under it goes straight back to cooling. That reader gets two promises before the first scroll.

4. Promise check

Up to five customer counts before she reads a paragraph

  • In the ad: trusted by 21,346+ hot sleepers.
  • On the ad's card: 21,000+ Happy Sleepers.
  • On the page's rotating top bar: Loved By 51,257+ Households.
  • Under the stars: 4.9 Average Rating from 2,500+ Happy Sleepers.
  • In headline version 2: 40,000+ Hot Sleepers.

Each number may be true on its own. Read in a row, they make her wonder which one is.

5. What I'd test first

In the order I'd run them. Every test runs against what the page does today, with traffic from the same ads split at random.

Test 1: give the night sweats ads their own first screen

Send these 8 ads to version 2 only, and test that against today's three-way split. Judged on purchases per visitor from these ads, run to its planned sample and end date.

Test 2: one count, everywhere

Pick one customer number and use it in the ad, the top bar, the rating line and the headline.

Test 3: open on the ad's own scene

The first image under the headline is a timeline chart. The ad's scene is waking up drenched. Test a first image that shows it.

These are hypotheses from public pages. Plufl's own numbers decide which one goes first.
