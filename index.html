The problem

The ad did its job. The page never collected on it.

It's late and Ads Manager is open. One ad has the cheapest clicks in the account: people stop on it, read it and tap. Its cost per purchase is still worse than ads with half its click-through rate. It's easy to call that the wrong audience or creative fatigue.

Here is what happens after the tap. She's on the couch at ten at night, thumb moving. Your ad stops her because it's about the one thing she has. It shows a woman her age with one problem and tells one story. She wants the rest of it, so she taps.

She lands on a page built for everyone who will ever buy the product. There's a gallery, a price and a buy box above the fold, then a list of benefits in which the one she clicked for comes fourth. She wanted to know what's wrong, how this fixes it, and whether women like her say it worked. She closes the tab.

So you do what the dashboard tells you. You brief new creatives, raise the budget and watch CPA climb, then cut back and brief again. The ad takes the blame for a problem that starts after the click.

The question is where the story continues, not "presell or product page". Sometimes the answer is a product page rebuilt around the angle. Sometimes it's an advertorial or listicle that answers what the ad left open and then hands her to the product page. A test against your current page settles each case.

Questions

What founders ask before they send an ad.

Straight to the product page works best for us.

It might. If it does, it stays. Your product page is the control, and nothing I build replaces it until it beats it in a randomized test. Sometimes the best answer is a product page rebuilt around one angle. If your best ads are long videos that already teach the mechanism, the work may be on the product page itself.

Show me a page you built that beat a control.

I don't have a published randomized win against a product page yet, and I won't dress one up. What you can judge today: one of your own ads, mapped before you pay; live pages I wrote, opened on a call; and the test rules we both sign before anything runs. There's no minimum term, and if nothing has beaten your control by the end of month three, month four is free.

Can you guarantee a lift?

Not a percentage, and be careful with anyone who promises one before seeing your data. What I do guarantee: if no page or test has beaten your control in a randomized test by the end of month three, month four is free, as long as each test runs to its planned sample and end date and the control stays unchanged while it runs.

We tried advertorials and listicles. They didn't work.

Ask three things about that test: which ads fed it, how long it ran, and what it was judged on. A copied listicle tested for three days and judged on platform attribution can read very differently in a split test. That's why only randomized tests count here.

Others review the store free on a call. Why pay $2,000?

You get the free part too: one of your ads, mapped before you decide. A call gives you opinions in 30 minutes. The diagnostic gives you a written lost-click map, a tracking check, a promise check from ad to checkout, your customers' own words, a claims sheet, three proven reference pages and a test plan that states the sample each test needs. You own it and can run it in-house.

Tests at our size are too small to prove anything.

They are if they test small things. At a 3% conversion rate, a test needs about 1,600 purchases per arm to find a 10% lift, and about 420 per arm to find a 20% lift (95% confidence, 80% power). A button colour can't be read at your traffic. A page that carries the ad's story can move enough to be read, which is why every test here is a big swing.

Do we have to give you ad account access?

Read access, or a monthly export from Ads Manager and Shopify. I never touch budgets or campaigns.

We're in health. What about compliance?

Every claim on the page has a source on the claims sheet. No invented doctors or publications. A soft label at the top and the full disclosure at the bottom. Nothing that lifts conversion while raising refunds or legal risk.
